\section{Tightness}
\label{sec.tightness}

We show two things. No constant confirmation depth is sound. And the depth
$\prn(r,\hgt)$ that \Cref{alg.client} prunes is, in the limit, no more than it
has to be. One attack gives both. The adversary follows the honest chain for
$r_0$ rounds, so that the chain she holds is as long as any chain the validators
hold. Then she stops publishing, mines for $s$ more rounds in private, and hands
the client what she has. The blocks she kept private are on no honest chain, and
there are about $\advr fs$ of them. Throughout this section the adversary has the
largest power that \Cref{asm.majority} allows, $t=\advr(n-t)$. The proofs are
in \Cref{appendix.tightness}.

\begin{definition}[Private mining]\label{def.private}
    The strategy $\pipriv$ with parameters $r_0\ge1$ and $s\ge1$ is the
    following. Write $r=r_0+s$.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item In every round of $[1,r_0]$, after the honest validators have
            sent their messages, the adversary spends every query on
            extending a longest chain among the genesis chain and the chains
            sent by any validator so far. Each block she computes extends the
            block she computed before in the same round. She sends every block
            at once.
        \item Let $\ch_0$ be a longest chain adopted by an honest validator at
            round $r_0+1$. In every round of $[r_0+1,r]$ the adversary spends
            every query on extending $\ch_0$, each block extending the one
            before, and sends nothing to the validators.
        \item At round $r$ she sends the client the chain $\ch$ formed by
            $\ch_0$ and every block she computed in the rounds of $[r_0+1,r]$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Tightness, informal]\label{thm.tight}
    Let the client receive at round $r$ the chain $\ch$ that $\pipriv$ sends it.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item For every constant $k$, once $s$ exceeds about $k/(\advr f)$,
            $\prune{\ch}{k}$ is a prefix of no chain adopted by an honest
            validator at round $r$, with overwhelming probability in
            $\min\{r_0,s\}$.
        \item With $s=\advr r$, the chain $\ch$ is about as tall as an honest
            chain, about $fr$, and about $\advr^2fr$ of its blocks are on no
            honest chain. This is what the client prunes from it.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\Cref{cor.noconstant,cor.ruletight} in \Cref{appendix.tightness} state both
items precisely. The second item matters for honest chains too. The attack chain has height
about $fr$, which is the height of an honest chain in an execution where the
adversary does not mine at all. A rule that reads only the round and the height
cannot tell the two executions apart, so it prunes about $\advr^2fr$ from the
honest chain too. That is the lag behind the honest chain in \Cref{thm.liveness},
and for rules of this kind it is necessary. A rule that reads timestamps, the
target, or the contents of blocks is outside the model of \Cref{sec.model}, and
we make no claim about it.
