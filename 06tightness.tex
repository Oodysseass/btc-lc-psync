\section{Tightness}
\label{sec.tightness}

We show two things. No constant confirmation depth is sound. And no safe rule
that reads only the round and the height prunes, in the limit, less than the
depth $\prn(r,\hgt)$ of \Cref{alg.client}. One attack gives both. The adversary
follows the honest chain for $r_0$ rounds, so that the chain she holds is as
long as any chain the validators hold. Then she stops publishing, mines for $s$
more rounds in private, and hands the client what she has, possibly cut at a
height of her choice. The blocks she kept private are on no honest chain, and
there are about $\advr fs$ of them. Throughout this section $\advr>0$ and the
adversary has the largest power that \Cref{asm.majority} allows,
$t=\advr(n-t)$. The proofs are in \Cref{appendix.tightness}.

\begin{definition}[Private mining]\label{def.private}
    The strategy $\pipriv$ with parameters $r_0\ge1$ and $s\ge1$ is the
    following. Write $r=r_0+s$.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item In every round of $[1,r_0]$, after the honest validators have
            sent their messages, the adversary spends every query on
            extending a longest chain among the genesis chain and the chains
            sent by any validator so far. Each block she computes extends the
            block she computed before in the same round. She sends every block
            at once.
        \item Let $\ch_0$ be a longest chain adopted by an honest validator at
            round $r_0+1$. In every round of $[r_0+1,r]$ the adversary spends
            every query on extending $\ch_0$, each block extending the one
            before, and sends nothing to the validators.
        \item At round $r$ she sends the client the chain $\ch$ formed by
            $\ch_0$ and every block she computed in the rounds of $[r_0+1,r]$.
    \end{enumerate}
    For $\hgt\ge0$, the strategy $\pipriv^\hgt$ is the same, except that at
    round $r$ she sends the client $\ch[{:}\hgt+1]$, the chain $\ch$ cut at
    height $\hgt$, whenever $\ch$ has height at least $\hgt$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Private mining]\label{thm.attack}
    Let $r_0,s\ge1$, let $r=r_0+s$ and let $\epsilon'\in(0,1)$. In an
    execution against $\pipriv$, except with probability at most
    $\typ+3e^{-\epsilon'^2\advr f\min\{r_0,s\}/3}$, the chain $\ch$ sent to
    the client is in $\bt_r$, and every chain $\ch'$ adopted by an honest
    validator at round $r$ satisfies
    \begin{align}
        \len{\ch}-\len{\ch\meet\ch'} &> (1-\epsilon')\advr fs,
            \label{eq.attfork}\\
        \len{\ch}-1 &> (1-\epsilon')\left[(1+\advr)fr_0+\advr fs\right].
            \label{eq.attheight}
    \end{align}
\end{lemma}

The first item of the next theorem says that no constant depth is safe. The
second gives a lower bound on the depth of every safe rule that reads only the
round and the height. The third says that the depth of \Cref{alg.client} meets
this bound in the limit, at every height.

\begin{theorem}[Tightness]\label{thm.tight}
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Let $\ch$ be the chain that $\pipriv$ sends the client at round
            $r$. Let $k\ge0$, $\epsilon'\in(0,1)$, $r_0\ge1$ and
            $s\ge\lceil k\rceil/\left[(1-\epsilon')\advr f\right]$. Except with
            probability at most $\typ+3e^{-\epsilon'^2\advr f\min\{r_0,s\}/3}$,
            $\prune{\ch}{k}$ is a prefix of no chain adopted by an honest
            validator at round $r$.
        \item Let $R$ be a function of the round and the height, and let a
            client prune $\lceil R(r,\hgt)\rceil$ blocks from each chain of
            height $\hgt$ that it receives at round $r$. Let
            $\epsilon'\in(0,1)$, $r_0,s\ge1$, $r=r_0+s$ and
            $\hgt\le(1-\epsilon')\left[(1+\advr)fr_0+\advr fs\right]$.
            Suppose that, against $\pipriv^\hgt$, the chain the client computes
            at round $r$ is a prefix of every chain adopted by an honest
            validator at round $r$ with probability greater than
            $\typ+3e^{-\epsilon'^2\advr f\min\{r_0,s\}/3}$. Then
            \[
                \lceil R(r,\hgt)\rceil>\hgt-\maxr f\max\{r_0,\lambda\} .
            \]
        \item Let $a\in(\advr,1+\advr)$, $\epsilon'\in(0,1-a/(1+\advr))$,
            $\hgt=\lfloor afr\rfloor$,
            $r_0=\lceil\hgt/((1-\epsilon')f)-\advr r\rceil$ and $s=r-r_0$. For
            large $r$, these meet the conditions of the second item on
            $r_0$, $s$ and $\hgt$, and
            \[
                \lim_{f,\epsilon,\epsilon'\to0}\ \lim_{r\to\infty}\
                \frac{\hgt-\maxr f\max\{r_0,\lambda\}}{fr}
                =\lim_{f,\epsilon,\epsilon'\to0}\ \lim_{r\to\infty}\
                \frac{\prn(r,\hgt)}{fr}
                =\advr(1+\advr-a) .
            \]
    \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The cut is what makes the second item hold at every height. Without it, the
height of the attack chain would be random, and a rule could prune much at the
heights that attacks produce and little at the others. Together, the second
and third items say that no safe rule that reads only the round and the height
prunes, in the limit, less than \Cref{alg.client}, at any height. This matters
for honest chains too. When the adversary does not mine, an honest chain at
round $r$ has height about $fr$, and at $a=1$ the third item gives
$\advr^2fr$. So every safe rule of this kind prunes about a $\advr^2$ fraction
of such a chain, as \Cref{alg.client} does (\Cref{sec.liveness}), and its
confirmation delay grows linearly with the round, as in \Cref{thm.liveness}. A
rule that reads timestamps, the target, or the contents of blocks is outside
the model of \Cref{sec.model}, and we make no claim about it.
