\section{Tightness}
\label{sec.tightness}

We show three things. The coefficient $\frac{t}{n-2t}$ of \Cref{thm.prefix}
cannot be lowered. No constant confirmation depth is sound. And the depth
$\prn(r,\hgt)$ that \Cref{def.client} prunes is, in the limit, no more than it has
to be. One attack gives all three. The adversary follows the honest chain for
$r_0$ rounds, so that the chain it holds is as long as any chain the
validators hold. Then it stops publishing, mines for $s$ more rounds in
private, and hands the client what it has. The blocks it kept private are on
no honest chain, and there are about $\advr fs$ of them.

\begin{definition}[Private mining]\label{def.private}
    The strategy $\pipriv$ with parameters $r_0\ge1$ and $s\ge1$ is the
    following. Write $r=r_0+s$.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item In every round of $[1,r_0]$, after the honest validators have
            sent their messages, the adversary spends every query on
            extending a longest chain among the genesis chain and the chains
            sent by any validator so far. Each block it computes extends the
            block it computed before in the same round. It sends every block
            at once.
        \item Let $\ch_0$ be a longest chain adopted by an honest validator at
            round $r_0+1$. In every round of $[r_0+1,r]$ the adversary spends
            every query on extending $\ch_0$, each block extending the one
            before, and sends nothing to the validators.
        \item At round $r$ it sends the client the chain $\ch$ formed by
            $\ch_0$ and every block it computed in the rounds of $[r_0+1,r]$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

A block computed at round $r$ may be extended by a query made in the same
round. \Cref{lem.length} counts adversarial blocks by $Z$, which counts
queries, so it already allows this.

\begin{theorem}[Private mining]\label{thm.attack}
    Let $r_0,s\ge1$, let $r=r_0+s$ and let $\epsilon'\in(0,1)$. In an
    execution against $\pipriv$, except with probability at most
    $\typ+8e^{-\epsilon'^2\advr f\min\{r_0,s\}/3}$, the chain $\ch$ sent to
    the client is in $\bt_r$, and every chain $\ch'$ adopted by an honest
    validator at round $r$ satisfies
    \begin{align}
        \len{\ch}-\len{\ch\meet\ch'} &> (1-\epsilon')\advr fs,
            \label{eq.attfork}\\
        \len{\ch'}-\len{\ch} &< \left[(1+\epsilon')-(1-\epsilon')\advr\right]fs,
            \label{eq.attupper}\\
        \len{\ch'}-\len{\ch} &> \left[(1-\epsilon')-(1+\epsilon')\tfrac{\advr}{1-f}\right]fs-1,
            \label{eq.attlower}\\
        (1-\epsilon')\left[(1+\advr)fr_0+\advr fs\right] < \len{\ch}-1
            &< (1+\epsilon')\left[\Big(1+\tfrac{\advr}{1-f}\Big)fr_0+\tfrac{\advr}{1-f}fs\right].
            \label{eq.attheight}
    \end{align}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    Write $S_1=[1,r_0]$, $S_2=[r_0+1,r]$ and $\ell_0=\len{\ch_0}$. We first
    establish four facts that hold in every typical execution, and then bound
    the random variables in them.

    First, $\ell_0=1+X(S_1)+Z(S_1)$, and every chain sent by round $r_0$ has
    length at most $\ell_0$. We show by induction on $r'\in[1,r_0+1]$ that
    every honest validator adopts at round $r'$ a chain of length
    $L_{r'}=1+X([1,r'-1])+Z([1,r'-1])$, and that the longest chain among the
    genesis chain and the chains sent by round $r'-1$ has length $L_{r'}$.
    Both hold at round $1$. Assume both at a round $r'\le r_0$. Honest
    validators send at round $r'$ chains of length at most $L_{r'}+X_{r'}$,
    and one of length $L_{r'}+1$ if $X_{r'}=1$. So, before the adversary acts,
    the longest chain among the genesis chain and the chains sent at rounds up
    to $r'$ has length $L_{r'}+X_{r'}$. The adversary extends it by $Z_{r'}$
    blocks and sends the result, of length $L_{r'+1}$. At round $r'+1$ every
    honest validator receives it, and the chain it has is at most
    $L_{r'}+X_{r'}$ long, so it adopts a chain of length $L_{r'+1}$. The case
    $r'=r_0+1$ is the claim.

    Second, in the rounds of $S_2$ the adversary queries only on the private
    branch, so $\len{\ch}=\ell_0+Z(S_2)$, and every block of $\ch$ was
    computed by round $r$, so $\ch\in\bt_r$.

    Third, the blocks the adversary computes in the rounds of $S_2$ go to
    the client only, so none lies on $\ch'$. Hence $\ch\meet\ch'\preceq\ch_0$
    and $\len{\ch}-\len{\ch\meet\ch'}\ge\len{\ch}-\ell_0=Z(S_2)$.

    Fourth, $\ell_0+X(S_2)-1\le\len{\ch'}\le\ell_0+X(S_2)$. The blocks of
    $\ch'$ computed in rounds of $S_2$ are honest and, by
    \Cref{lem.honestone}, at most $X(S_2)$ in number. The other blocks of
    $\ch'$ form a prefix of $\ch'$, since no predictions occur, and that
    prefix is the genesis chain or a prefix of a chain sent at the round its
    tip was computed, so it has length at most $\ell_0$ by the first fact.
    This gives the upper bound. For the lower bound, if $s=1$ then
    $\len{\ch'}=\ell_0$ by the first fact and $X(S_2)\le1$. If $s\ge2$, some
    honest validator has a chain of length at least $\ell_0+X_{r_0+1}$ at
    round $r_0+2$, and \Cref{lem.growth} from round $r_0+2$ to round $r$ gives
    $\len{\ch'}\ge\ell_0+X([r_0+1,r-1])\ge\ell_0+X(S_2)-1$.

    Now the randomness. $X(S_1)$ and $X(S_2)$ are sums of $r_0$ and $s$
    independent Bernoulli trials with success probability $f$. $Z(S_1)$ and
    $Z(S_2)$ are sums of $tqr_0$ and $tqs$ independent Bernoulli trials with
    success probability $p$, and \Cref{eq.rates} gives
    $\advr fr_0<pqtr_0<\frac{\advr}{1-f}fr_0$ and
    $\advr fs<pqts<\frac{\advr}{1-f}fs$. By the Chernoff bounds and
    $\advr<1$, each of the events
    \[
        \begin{gathered}
            X(S_2)\le(1-\epsilon')fs,\qquad X(S_2)\ge(1+\epsilon')fs,\\
            Z(S_2)\le(1-\epsilon')\advr fs,\qquad
            Z(S_2)\ge(1+\epsilon')\tfrac{\advr}{1-f}fs
        \end{gathered}
    \]
    has probability at most $e^{-\epsilon'^2\advr fs/3}$, and each of the four
    corresponding events for $X(S_1)$ and $Z(S_1)$, with $r_0$ in place of
    $s$, has probability at most $e^{-\epsilon'^2\advr fr_0/3}$. By a union
    bound, except with the stated probability the execution is typical and
    none of the eight events occurs. Then the third and fourth facts give
    \Cref{eq.attfork,eq.attupper,eq.attlower}, and the first two facts give
    \Cref{eq.attheight}, since $\len{\ch}-1=X(S_1)+Z(S_1)+Z(S_2)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Tightness]\label{cor.tight}
    Let $0\le c<\frac{t}{n-2t}$ and $b\ge0$. There are $\epsilon'\in(0,1)$ and
    $s_0$, depending only on $c$, $b$, $\advr$ and $f$, such that for every
    $r_0\ge1$ and $s\ge s_0$, in an execution against $\pipriv$, except with
    probability at most $\typ+8e^{-\epsilon'^2\advr f\min\{r_0,s\}/3}$, every
    chain $\ch'$ adopted by an honest validator at round $r$ satisfies
    $\len{\ch'}>\len{\ch}$ and
    \[
        \len{\ch}-\len{\ch\meet\ch'} > c\left(\len{\ch'}-\len{\ch}\right)+b .
    \]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
    Since $c<\advr/(1-\advr)$, we have $\advr(1+c)>c$. From \Cref{lem.rho},
    $\advr/(1-f)<1$. Choose $\epsilon'\in(0,1)$ small enough that
    \[
        \delta_1=(1-\epsilon')\advr(1+c)-c(1+\epsilon')>0
        \quad\text{and}\quad
        \delta_2=(1-\epsilon')-(1+\epsilon')\frac{\advr}{1-f}>0 .
    \]
    Consider an execution in which
    \Cref{eq.attfork,eq.attupper,eq.attlower} hold. From
    \Cref{eq.attfork,eq.attupper},
    \[
        \len{\ch}-\len{\ch\meet\ch'}-c\left(\len{\ch'}-\len{\ch}\right)
        > (1-\epsilon')\advr fs-c\left[(1+\epsilon')-(1-\epsilon')\advr\right]fs
        = \delta_1fs .
    \]
    From \Cref{eq.attlower}, $\len{\ch'}-\len{\ch}>\delta_2fs-1$. Take
    $s_0\ge\max\{b/(\delta_1f),\ 1/(\delta_2f)\}$. For $s\ge s_0$ the two
    right-hand sides are at least $b$ and $0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No constant depth]\label{cor.noconstant}
    Let $k\ge0$, let $\epsilon'\in(0,1)$, let $r_0\ge1$ and let
    $s\ge\lceil k\rceil/\left[(1-\epsilon')\advr f\right]$. In an execution
    against $\pipriv$, except with probability at most
    $\typ+8e^{-\epsilon'^2\advr f\min\{r_0,s\}/3}$, the chain $\ch$ the
    client receives at round $r$ satisfies $\prune{\ch}{k}\not\preceq\ch'$
    for every chain $\ch'$ adopted by an honest validator at round $r$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
    In an execution in which \Cref{eq.attfork} holds,
    $\len{\ch}-\len{\ch\meet\ch'}>(1-\epsilon')\advr fs\ge\lceil k\rceil$. The
    chain $\ch\meet\ch'$ contains the genesis block, so
    $\len{\ch}>\lceil k\rceil+1$ and $\prune{\ch}{k}$ has length
    $\len{\ch}-\lceil k\rceil>\len{\ch\meet\ch'}$. Both are prefixes of $\ch$,
    so $\prune{\ch}{k}$ is not a prefix of $\ch\meet\ch'$, and hence not of
    $\ch'$.
\end{proof}

A client that prunes a fixed number of blocks is therefore unsafe once the
adversary has mined in private for long enough. A client running
\Cref{def.client} prunes $\prn(r,\hgt)$, which grows with the round. The last
corollary says that it does not grow more than it must.

\begin{corollary}[The rule is tight]\label{cor.ruletight}
    Fix $\sigma\in(0,1)$, let $s=\sigma r$ and $r_0=(1-\sigma)r$, and let
    $\ch$ be the chain $\pipriv$ sends the client at round $r$. In an
    execution in which \Cref{eq.attfork,eq.attheight} hold,
    \[
        \lim_{f,\epsilon,\epsilon'\to0}\ \lim_{r\to\infty}\
        \frac{\prn(r,\len{\ch})}{\len{\ch}-\len{\ch\meet\ch'}} = 1
    \]
    for every chain $\ch'$ adopted by an honest validator at round $r$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
    The ratio is above $1$ by \Cref{thm.round}, which gives
    $\len{\ch}-\len{\ch\meet\ch'}<\prn(r,\len{\ch})$. For the other direction, divide
    \Cref{eq.attheight} by $fr$ and let $r\to\infty$ and then $f,\epsilon'\to0$:
    the height of $\ch$ in units of $fr$ tends to
    $(1+\advr)(1-\sigma)+\advr\sigma=1+\advr-\sigma$. By \Cref{cor.limit},
    $\prn(r,\len{\ch})/(fr)$ then tends to $\advr(1+\advr-\rate)=\advr\sigma$. By
    \Cref{eq.attfork}, $(\len{\ch}-\len{\ch\meet\ch'})/(fr)>(1-\epsilon')\advr\sigma$,
    which tends to $\advr\sigma$. So the ratio tends to at most $1$.
\end{proof}

Take $\sigma=\advr$. The attack chain then has height about $fr$, which is
the height of an honest chain in an execution where the adversary does not
mine at all, and the client prunes about $\advr^2fr$ from it. A rule that
reads only the round and the height cannot tell the two executions apart, so
it prunes $\advr^2fr$ from the honest chain too. That is the lag
\Cref{thm.liveness} charges an honest sender, and for rules of this kind it is
necessary. A rule that reads timestamps, the target, or the contents of blocks
is outside the model of \Cref{sec.model}, and we make no claim about it.
