\section{Tightness}
\label{sec.tightness}

In this section we show that the pruning depth of \Cref{alg.client} is
optimal, in the limit, among rules that read only the round and the height. We
also show that no constant confirmation depth is safe for a client. Both
results use the private mining attack below. Throughout this section
$\advr=t/(n-t)>0$, so the adversary has the largest mining power that
\Cref{asm.majority} allows. The proofs are in \Cref{appendix.tightness}.

In the attack, the adversary first mines on the public chain for $r_0$ rounds
and publishes her blocks at once. The honest chain then grows at a rate of
about $(1+\advr)f$. Next, she mines on this chain in private for $s$ rounds,
and sends the result to the client. This is the chain of \Cref{fig.rates}(b).

\begin{definition}[Private mining]\label{def.private}
    Let $r_0,s\ge1$ be integers and $r=r_0+s$. The strategy $\pipriv$ with parameters
    $r_0$ and $s$ is the following.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item In every round of $[1,r_0]$, after the honest validators have
            sent their messages, the adversary spends every query on extending
            a longest chain among the genesis chain and the chains sent by any
            party so far, each block extending the one before. She sends every
            block to all validators at once.
        \item Let $\ch_0$ be a longest chain adopted by an honest validator at
            round $r_0+1$. In every round of $[r_0+1,r]$ the adversary spends
            every query on extending $\ch_0$, each block extending the one
            before. She sends nothing to the validators.
        \item At round $r$ she sends the client the chain $\ch$ formed by
            $\ch_0$ and her blocks of the rounds of $[r_0+1,r]$.
    \end{enumerate}
    For $\hgt\in\mathbb{N}$, the strategy $\pipriv^\hgt$ is the same, except that at
    round $r$ she sends the client $\ch[{:}\hgt+1]$, the chain $\ch$ cut at
    height $\hgt$, whenever $\ch$ has height at least $\hgt$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Private mining]\label{lem.attack}
    Let $r_0,s\ge1$ be integers, $r=r_0+s$, $\epsilon'\in(0,1)$ and
    $\eta=\typ+3e^{-\epsilon'^2\advr f\min\{r_0,s\}/3}$, and let $\ch_0$ and
    $\ch$ be as in \Cref{def.private}. Against $\pipriv$, except with
    probability at most $\eta$, the execution is typical, for every chain
    $\ch'$ adopted by an honest validator at round $r$ we have
    $\ch\meet\ch'\preceq\ch_0$, and
    \begin{align}
        \len{\ch}-\len{\ch_0} &> (1-\epsilon')\advr fs,
            \label{eq.attfork}\\
        \len{\ch}-1 &> (1-\epsilon')\left[(1+\advr)fr_0+\advr fs\right].
            \label{eq.attheight}
    \end{align}
\end{lemma}

In \Cref{lem.attack}, $\epsilon'$ is the relative error of three Chernoff
bounds, and $\eta$ tends to $\typ$ as $r_0$ and $s$ grow.

\paragraph{Constant depth.}

By \Cref{eq.attfork}, the private part of $\ch$ has more than
$(1-\epsilon')\advr fs$ blocks. A client that prunes a constant number of
blocks $k$ therefore keeps a private block once $s$ is large enough.

\begin{corollary}[No constant depth]\label{cor.constant}
    Let $k\ge0$ be an integer, and let $r_0$, $s$, $r$, $\epsilon'$ and
    $\eta$ be as in \Cref{lem.attack}, with $(1-\epsilon')\advr fs\ge k$.
    Against $\pipriv$, except with probability at most
    $\eta$, a client that prunes $k$ blocks from the chain $\ch$ that $\pipriv$
    sends it at round $r$ computes a chain that
    is a prefix of no chain adopted by an honest validator at round $r$.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Lower bound.}

A \emph{rule} is a function $R$ that maps a round $r$ and a height $\hgt$ to a
number of blocks $R(r,\hgt)\in\mathbb{N}$. A client \emph{runs} $R$ if it
computes $\prune{\ch}{R(r,\hgt)}$ from each chain $\ch$ of height $\hgt$ that it
receives at round $r$. \Cref{alg.client} runs $\lceil\prn\rceil$.

If $\hgt$ is at most the right-hand side of \Cref{eq.attheight}, then against
$\pipriv^\hgt$ the client receives a chain of height $\hgt$. Every block of
this chain above $\ch_0$ is private. By \Cref{lem.length}, $\ch_0$ has height
less than $\maxr fr_0$ when $r_0\ge\lambda$. So a safe rule prunes more than
$\hgt-\maxr fr_0$ blocks.

\begin{theorem}[Lower bound]\label{thm.lower}
    Let $R$ be a rule, and let $r_0$, $s$, $r$, $\epsilon'$ and $\eta$ be
    as in \Cref{lem.attack}, with $r_0\ge\lambda$. Let $\hgt\in\mathbb{N}$ be
    at most the right-hand side of \Cref{eq.attheight}, with
    $R(r,\hgt)\le\hgt-\maxr fr_0$. Against $\pipriv^\hgt$, except with
    probability at most $\eta$, a client running $R$ computes at round $r$ a
    chain that is a prefix of no chain adopted by an honest validator at round
    $r$.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Comparison with the construction.}

The adversary takes the smallest $r_0$ for which her chain reaches height
$\hgt$. Her chain has height about $(1+\advr)fr_0+\advr fs=f(r_0+\advr r)$, so
$r_0\approx\hgt/f-\advr r$. The bound of \Cref{thm.lower} is then about
\(\hgt-(1+\advr)fr_0\approx\advr\left((1+\advr)fr-\hgt\right)\), which is
\Cref{eq.idealdepth}. \Cref{thm.tight} states this precisely. It normalizes
heights by $fr$, the height of an honest chain, and takes the limit of large
$r$ and small $f$, $\epsilon$ and $\epsilon'$. In this limit $\maxr$ and
$\advrone$ tend to $1+\advr$ and $\advr$.

\begin{theorem}[Tightness]\label{thm.tight}
    Let $a\in(\advr,1+\advr)$ and $\epsilon'\in(0,1-a/(1+\advr))$. For a
    round $r$, let $\hgt=\lfloor afr\rfloor$,
    $r_0=\lceil\hgt/((1-\epsilon')f)-\advr r\rceil$ and $s=r-r_0$. For every
    large $r$, these meet the conditions of \Cref{thm.lower} at height $\hgt$,
    and
    \[
        \lim_{f,\epsilon,\epsilon'\to0}\ \lim_{r\to\infty}\
            \frac{\hgt-\maxr fr_0}{fr}
        =\lim_{f,\epsilon\to0}\ \lim_{r\to\infty}\ \frac{\prn(r,\hgt)}{fr}
        =\advr(1+\advr-a) .
    \]
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Honest chains.}

When the adversary does not mine, a chain adopted by an honest validator at
round $r$ has height about $fr$, so $a=1$. A rule prunes the same number of
blocks from this chain as from the chain of the same height sent by
$\pipriv^\hgt$. Hence every safe rule prunes, in the limit, at least a
$\advr^2$ fraction of an honest chain, which is what \Cref{alg.client}
prunes. A transaction that enters the honest chain at round
$r'$ lies at height about $fr'$. At round $r$ a safe rule keeps a chain of
height at most about $(1-\advr^2)fr$, so it confirms the transaction only
after about $\advr^2r'/(1-\advr^2)$ rounds, as \Cref{alg.client} does
(\Cref{sec.liveness}). Our results do not cover rules that
also read timestamps, the target or the contents of blocks, since these are
outside the model of \Cref{sec.model}.
