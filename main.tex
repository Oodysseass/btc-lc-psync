\title{Bitcoin Clients Without Synchrony}
\author{Anonymous Submission}
\institute{}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Bitcoin is a synchronous proof of work consensus protocol. Its resilience
against an adversary holding just under half of the mining power rests on
synchrony among the validators, and that assumption is known to be necessary.
Clients are a different kind of party. A client does not mine and does not take
part in the protocol. It only wants to read a ledger. Wallets are clients, and
a client often has a poor connection, or would rather not delegate its view of
the chain to a third party. The decentralised alternatives, simplified payment
verification and the succinct proofs that followed it, all rest on the same two
assumptions: that at least one party the client connects to is honest, and that
the network is synchronous. We remove both. Our observation is that the honest
validators grow the chain at a rate the client can compute in advance. A chain
that is too short for the round in which it arrives is evidence of a fork that
was mined in private, and the same rate bounds how long that fork can be. From
the chain and a clock, and from nothing else, the client computes a prefix that
is safe against every adversary. No party it speaks to need be honest, and no
message need ever arrive on time. This is not the sleepy client model, in which
a party knows when it was asleep and assumes synchrony while awake. Our client
assumes nothing at all about the network and holds only a clock, which counts
the rounds since the execution began. We give the construction, prove it safe,
and prove it live: the client keeps confirming new transactions, at a rate we
bound. We then prove the bound tight, by exhibiting an attack under which no
client can confirm a longer prefix.
\end{abstract}
