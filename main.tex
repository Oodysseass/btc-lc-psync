\title{Bitcoin Clients Without Synchrony}
\author{Anonymous Submission}
\institute{}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Bitcoin's $k$-deep confirmation rule is proved for parties that mine and are
synchronised with one another. A client is neither. It cannot check that the
peer it asks for a chain is honest, and it has no bound on how long it must
wait for one that is. We show that no constant confirmation depth is sound for
such a client, and we give the rule that replaces it. Take the Bitcoin backbone
model with $n$ parties, of which $t$ are Byzantine. A client holding a chain
that falls $d$ blocks short of a chain held by an honest validator need discard
only $\frac{t}{n-2t}d$ blocks plus a constant, and no smaller coefficient
suffices. A client does not know the length of an honest chain. It measures its
own chain against the round instead, and discards $\frac{t}{n-t}$ of the gap to
the tallest height any chain can have reached. The output is a prefix of
every chain an honest validator adopts from that round onwards, and the outputs
of any two clients are prefixes of one another. None of this assumes anything
about the client's network, and a client that never hears an honest message is
still safe. Liveness is what the network costs: a client that receives at round
$r$ a chain as long as the honest chain of $D$ rounds earlier confirms the
honest chain up to round
$\left(1-\frac{t^2}{(n-t)^2}\right)r-\frac{n}{n-t}D$. A client with an honest peer
therefore still pays a $\frac{t^2}{(n-t)^2}$ fraction of the age of the chain.
That is about $1.2\%$ against an adversary holding a tenth of the mining power,
and $25\%$ against one holding a third. A private mining adversary
attains the coefficient.
\end{abstract}
