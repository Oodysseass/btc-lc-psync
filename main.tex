\title{Intergalactic Consensus:\\Asynchronous Bitcoin Clients}
\author{Anonymous Submission}
\institute{}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Bitcoin is a \emph{synchronous} proof of work consensus protocol. Its resilience
against an adversary holding just under half of the mining power rests exactly
on the synchrony among the validators, and that assumption is known to be
necessary. This assumption is also transferred to clients, where all client
constructions up until now assume synchrony and existential honesty among
their peers. However, clients can and should be treated in a more robust model.
We remove both assumptions and achieve this. Our construction allows for a
client to verify payments even if it has a poor connection, or may rather not
trust a third party for a view of the chain.

Our observation is that the honest validators grow the chain at a rate the
client can compute in advance. A chain that is too short for the round in which
it arrives is evidence of a fork that was mined in private, and the same rate
bounds how long that fork can be. From the chain and a clock, and from nothing
else, the client computes a prefix that is safe against every adversary; no
party it speaks to need be honest, and no message need ever arrive on time. This
is not the sleepy client model, in which a party knows when it was asleep and
assumes synchrony while awake. Our client assumes nothing at all about the
network and holds only a clock, which counts the rounds since the execution
began. We give the construction, prove it safe, and prove it live: the client
keeps confirming new transactions, at a rate we bound. We then prove the bound
tight, by exhibiting an attack under which no client can confirm a longer
prefix.
\end{abstract}
