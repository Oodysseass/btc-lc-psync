\title{Intergalactic Consensus:\\Asynchronous Bitcoin Clients}
\author{Anonymous Submission}
\institute{}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Bitcoin is a \emph{synchronous} proof of work consensus protocol. Its security
against a minority adversary rests on the synchrony among the validators, and
that assumption is known to be necessary. Bitcoin clients have inherited it:
every client construction to date assumes that messages arrive on time and that
at least one of its peers is honest. We show that a client needs neither.  We
keep the validators synchronous and give the client an asynchronous link to
them, over which the adversary may delay or drop any message and may itself
answer. The client holds a clock and nothing else. Bitcoin confirms a block once
it is $k$ blocks deep in the chain, for a constant $k$. Our client confirms a
block once it is deep enough for the current round and for the height of the
chain it received. We prove the rule safe and live in the Bitcoin backbone
model: what the client confirms is a prefix of every honest chain, at the round
of receipt and at every round after, and the confirmed prefix grows at a
constant rate. Asynchrony costs latency. A client that receives an honest chain
at round $r$ discards a $t^2/(n-t)^2$ fraction of it, where $t$ of the $n$
validators are adversarial. It discards $\Theta(r)$ blocks where a synchronous
client discards a constant, and it confirms a block $\Theta(r)$ rounds after the
honest validators do. We prove this tight: a private mining adversary forces
every safe rule to discard as much, and no constant depth is safe.
\end{abstract}
